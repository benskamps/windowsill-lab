# Verdict — what two days tested, and what windowsill should be

**2026-08-26.** Written at Ben's request, in closing.

## The question actually being asked

> *"Can we find holes in what exists and quickly fill them with our names?"*

That was the real experiment. Everything else — the doctrine, the contracts, the
pots, the sweeps — was instrumentation for it. Here is the answer the evidence
gives.

## Eight holes, tested

| # | hole | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | HP degeneracy table beyond the published edge | **filled** — edge at N≈19, we'd arrive behind it, field moved to real proteins |
| 2 | attribution-topology gaps | **untestable** — citation graphs are untyped; 3 of 4 signatures uncomputable |
| 3 | arithmetic errors in physics papers | **nothing to check** — 50 papers, 2 recomputable claims, both correct. `statcheck` works because APA *mandates* the numbers; physics doesn't |
| 4 | saturation bias in susceptibility | **known** — multiple field values is standard practice; we violated it |
| 5 | stale edges via stated constraints | **narrow** — 10% actionable, and only where compute binds. "Was it already resolved?" is unanswerable |
| 6 | future-work mining | **2%** — "future work" is the authors' own next paper. Territory claimed, not released |
| 7 | fringe fields | **no** — Kozyrev n=2, EmDrive n=23. And psi/UAP are growing *faster* than the index |
| 8 | pentimento (results removed in revision) | **hypothesis dead** — papers net *gain* numbers, 485/391 |

**Seven of eight killed.** The one survivor is not a hole at all.

## Why the shortcut doesn't exist

**The holes that are cheap to find are cheap for everyone, so they are filled.**
What remains needs one of two things we cannot buy: **domain fluency** — which
cost four GPU-hours the day it was tested, comparing a linear-response
susceptibility against published *fluctuation* exponents without noticing — or
**sustained infrastructure**, which this box demonstrably cannot hold. Every
corpus attempt across two days stalled: the attribution graph, the Crossref pull
(0% on-topic), the arXiv bulk harvest (zero bytes).

That is not a morale problem. It is a measurement, and it took two days instead
of two years.

## What actually worked, every single time

**Small decisive measurements.** Under an hour, one number, verdict either way:

* dt convergence — 0.04% across the sweep · clip audit — innocent at 250×
* multi-version rate — 49.5% (n=1200) · revision deltas — 485 added / 391 removed
* placebo null — 3 exceedances in 84,500 draws · exact fold degeneracy — 17,466 sequences
* limit audit of A02 — worst bias 0.20% of its own tolerance

**Eight for eight.** The instrument that works here is a well-posed question
answered in an afternoon. The instrument that fails is anything requiring a
corpus, an audience, or a judgment about a field.

## The one thing I got wrong in closing, and it matters

I filed the revision study as a **null** because it killed my hypothesis. It is
not a null:

> *Papers grow between arXiv v1 and final — median +690 words, 40 of 45 — and
> gain numerical content (485 vs 391). 29% net-lose, with a severe tail: one
> paper shed 14,030 words including a contiguous 4,880-word passage.*

That is a measurement of **what peer review does to a paper**. Novel, checkable
by anyone with two PDFs, no domain expertise, and scalable to n=500 in three
hours with the tool already written. **I dismissed it because it wasn't the
result I asked for** — the same asymmetry I spent two days hunting, one layer up.

## Verdict on windowsill

**Not decommissioned. Not expanded. Narrowed.**

**Keep**, because it costs nothing and produces honest receipts:
the campaign lane, the hunt lane, the deep lane (which correctly reported idle
rather than manufacturing work), the checkers, the scramble null (312k draws),
the objection ledger, the convergence auditor.

**Freeze**: the frontier machinery. The migration order has eight unbuilt items
and **not one makes an artifact more likely to be used by anyone.** Twelve layers
of anti-fooling apparatus around a lab with four unique visitors a fortnight is
the wrong ratio, and it was the wrong ratio before today.

**Spend attention only on**: one small decisive measurement at a time, each
finishable in a day, each with a stated kill condition. That is the only mode
with an unbroken record here.

**Stop entirely**: corpus building, adjudicating live disputes between
specialists, and any project whose value depends on an audience we do not have.

## The thing that would change the answer

Not a better method. **A reader.** Every mechanism built this week buys ordering,
code-path separation, artifact existence, and decorrelated weights — and not one
of them is a second mind that wants a different answer. Four unique visitors in
fourteen days, at least two of them Ben.

The lab is not short of rigour. It is short of anyone to be rigorous *at*. That
is not a lab problem and no amount of instrumentation touches it.
