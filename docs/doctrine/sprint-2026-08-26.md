### What A1 killed

My first attempt sampled **this week's** submissions and measured 1% — a
sampling error, not a result. Recent papers have not had time to be revised.
Aged sampling gives **49.5%**.

### What A2 found, and the honest limit

Version-specific text is retrievable. But **half of all abstracts are
byte-identical across three versions**, and the changes that exist are mostly
expansion. **The pentimento is in the body, not the abstract** — which makes
this a PDF-fetching project rather than an API project, at ~8s per paper.

### A3 — the hypothesis is dead, and the tail is the real object

**n = 45, v1 vs final version:**

```
words gained     median +690      40/45 papers grew
numbers REMOVED  total 391
numbers ADDED    total 485        net +94
net LOSE numbers 13/45 = 29%
net GAIN numbers 23/45 = 51%
unchanged         9/45 = 20%
among papers that grew >200 words: only 30% lose numbers
```

**Papers do not systematically shed results in revision. They net gain
numbers.** The hypothesis I stated two hours ago — that revision strips results —
is wrong at this sample size and wrong in the opposite direction. It came from a
two-paper hand sample that pointed the wrong way, which is why n=2 is never a
measurement.

**But 29% net-lose, and the extreme tail is real and verified.** The largest:

| paper | versions | words | decimal values | largest single deletion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2306.16582** | v1→v4 | **32,868 → 18,838** (−14,030) | 28 → 14 | **4,880 words** |
| 2306.14294 | v1→v3 | −494 | 45 → 26 (−20 +1) | |
| 2306.14747 | v1→v3 | +475 | 21 → 10 (−11 +0) | |
| 2306.17220 | v1→v3 | +1,807 | 27 → 17 (−12 +2) | |

2306.16582 was checked against arXiv directly: four real versions, and a single
contiguous ~4,900-word passage — a derivation on imaginary-time propagators —
removed between v1 and v4. That is a genuine pentimento: a whole section painted
over.

**So the finding is a null with a tail.** The population question ("do papers
lose results?") is answered no. The interesting object is the ~29% that do, and
especially the handful that shed 10–20 values or thousands of words. **A4 should
be re-pointed at the tail rather than the average**, and the honest framing is no
longer "science hides results" but "a minority of papers are radically
restructured between submission and publication, and nobody has looked at what
comes out."

**Noise check:** if `pdftotext` artifacts dominated, removals and additions
should both inflate. Additions exceed removals by 94, so extraction noise is not
producing a spurious loss signal — if anything it argues the tail is real.

### The caveat that rides on every number here

`pdftotext` on LaTeX-heavy physics is noisy; equation rendering differs between
compilations, so some "removed" values are extraction artifacts. **That biases
raw removals upward by an unknown amount.** The defence is that the same noise
applies to *additions*, so a systematic gap between the two is harder to explain
away than either count alone. Any claim from this must be comparative.
