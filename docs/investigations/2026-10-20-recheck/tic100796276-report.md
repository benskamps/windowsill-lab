**Whose:** Pavel Rabtsevich's, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23509748. This is an
independent re-check by a different instrument. It is not our discovery and
claims none.
**Pre-registration:** `docs/preregistrations/2026-10-20-recheck-tic100796276-tic4206066.md`

**Consumer:** Ben and the project thread, once, to decide whether to reply to the author. A one-off investigation, not a recurring surface; it retires nothing.
**Receipt:** `tic100796276-receipt.json` in this directory. It pins every FITS

## Verdict, as pre-registered

**Machine disposition: `low-significance`** under the pre-registered rule
("any claimed event with data but no dip ≥ 7 → this instrument does not
reproduce it").

That word comes from **one event only**: his second predicted transit,
2026-06-05, which exists only as a QLP light curve (S104). That light curve was
admitted by Amendment 1, and its noise is far from Gaussian (see below). On the
SPOC 2-min and TESS-SPOC data the pre-registration names as primary, **every
test agrees with him and no gate fires.** Both statements are true, and the
second does not overrule the first: the rule was fixed before the data, and it
is reported as it came out.

Our depths run about 12 % below his 0.32 %. That is the expected direction:
the 0.5-day running-median detrend and a box estimator both bias a 2.4-h
transit's depth low. Depths are compared here, not graded, as pre-registered.

## What does not agree, or did not come out clean

1. **2026-06-05 (S104, QLP, predicted).** At the predicted time the box depth
   is 1,648 ± 266 ppm (6.2σ), and the box χ² mid-time sits +10 min from his
   ephemeris. So *something* is there, at the right time. But it is about 0.6×
   the depth seen in the other four sectors, and the single-event scan's SNR is
   below 7, which is the line that failed it. That sector's own brightening
   null is **17.9**, against 3.9–5.0 everywhere else, so its noise is
   dominated by something non-Gaussian; he notes strong stray light at the
   start of S104. A half-depth dip in a disturbed QLP sector is weak support,
   and the pre-registered rule reads it as "not reproduced". We read it the
   same way.
2. **The 2020-07-06 transit in the 10-min TESS-SPOC copy of S27** (the
   cross-check tier). There is no scan dip ≥ 7 there, while the 2-min SPOC copy
   of the same event has one at SNR 7.2 and the box depth in the 10-min copy is
   7.5σ. This is the same photons at coarser cadence, and the cross-check tier
   does not enter the verdict. It is recorded because it shows how close to the
   scan's line these single events sit.
3. **The secondary-eclipse bound does not bite.** Phase 0.5 gives 229 ± 409 ppm.
   The 3σ ceiling at any phase is 1,515 ppm, over 95 % of the orbit. Against a
   ~2,740 ppm primary that bounds the companion-to-host surface-brightness
   ratio at < 0.55, which means a companion cooler than about 3,135 K, and that
   **does not exclude a stellar companion** (the line is 2,300 K). This
   instrument cannot reproduce his tighter "≲ 0.05 %" secondary limit. That is
   a statement about our sensitivity, not about his number.
4. **The centroid shift is near the line.** It is 2.86σ against a 3σ gate, on
   only three events, but the implied offset is a quarter of the 0.5-px bar.
   With three events the bootstrap error is itself rough. Reported, not graded
   further.

## What could not be tested

- **2026-06-23 (S105).** No archived light curve of any kind exists.
- **The pixel-level localisation** ("within ~5″"). This lab has no
  difference-image tool, so it is not reproduced.
- **An unresolved companion within ~1 px that the TIC does not list.** Nothing
  in TESS photometry, his or ours, closes this channel. It needs
  high-resolution imaging, Gaia DR4 astrometry (2026-12-02) or RVs.
- **His TRICERATOPS / MOLUSC numbers.** They were not re-run.

## Reading

An independent instrument, blind to his period, finds **18.152 d** as the top
period from the three events in SPOC and TESS-SPOC data. It also confirms his
timing to ≤ 11 min across five events, his odd/even agreement and his reading
of SPOC's 11.47 d alias, and it finds no gate that fires. Under the rule fixed
in advance it does **not** reproduce all of his events. The one it does not is
his second predicted transit, which sits at the right time but at roughly half
depth, in a QLP sector whose noise is about four times worse than anywhere else
on this star. The open alternative he names, a planet around an unresolved
companion, is untouched by anything here.
