# TIC 374861595 — the 1.937 d "open lead" is most likely an eclipsing binary at 3.874 d

**Status: REFUTED, on outside review and then on the lab's own light curves (pending
Ben's ruling — merging this file and the `docs/shelf-rulings.json` row beside it is the
ruling).** The reasons arrived in
[issue #189](https://github.com/benskamps/windowsill-lab/issues/189), opened
2026-11-06 by GitHub user `hposborn`, who writes there as a TOI vetter and
exoplanet astronomer. He had come to the repo looking for the ExoFOP policy
change, which the lab documents, and read some of the analysis of this star.

On 2026-11-10 the lab ran its own fold gates and one new measurement on all 24
light curves it already had on disk. Its data agree with him. **No new data was
pulled and Gaia was not re-queried.** The lab has not measured a mass and has not
confirmed a binary. "Most likely" is the claim.

The star had been on the shelf since 2026-09-01 and `promotable-awaiting-ben`
since 2026-09-12, with the line "every machine criterion is clear" (as
`PYTHONPATH=src python -m lab.cli shelf` printed it at `3c25530`). It was the only
entry the shelf called promotable.

## What the issue says

Two numbered reasons and a closing observation, in the vetter's order. Read the
issue for his wording and his plot.

1. **The radius is implausible for the host.** A planet twice Jupiter's size
   needs heating an M dwarf cannot supply. Planets that large are found around
   G, F and A stars.
2. **There is variability at twice the planetary period.** He reads the
   low-significance odd/even difference as a hint of a circular,
   close-to-equal-mass binary at 2P. Tides, Doppler beaming and reflection all
   make coherent signals at a binary's own period, and he says that signal is
   clearly present here, and shows a plot. It is lost, he notes, when the light
   curve is detrended and the transits are studied one at a time. On this
   reading the odd dips are the secondary eclipse and the even dips the primary,
   which is why no separate secondary is seen.

He adds that the binary reading also explains why the Gaia radial-velocity error
is so high (he gives 8.36 km/s), and that TOI vetters have seen this target a lot
and never promoted it.

## What the lab measured on 2026-11-10

All 24 two-minute light curves were on disk from the 2026-09-18 refit download
(sectors 27 to 97; the three graded sectors hash to their receipts).
`scripts/tic374861595_p2_fold_all_sectors.py` ran three things on each, on one
ephemeris (SPOC's), and wrote
[`2026-11-10-tic374861595-p2-fold-24-sectors.json`](2026-11-10-tic374861595-p2-fold-24-sectors.json).
Every figure in this section is in that file.

**1. The light between the dips keeps step with twice the dip period.** With
every dip masked and no detrending, a sinusoid at 2P is present in all 24
sectors, with a semi-amplitude between 0.11 % and 2.10 %. In the 16 sectors from
2023 to 2025 (sectors 61 to 97, 255 cycles of 2P) the phase of maximum light
stays between 0.47 and 0.60 of a cycle. A straight line through those phases
gives a modulation period of **3.87411 ± 0.00039 d. Twice the dip period is
3.87390 d.** In 2020 and 2021 the phase sat elsewhere and moved between seasons
(0.34 and 0.23, then 0.97 and 0.96, then 0.88 to 0.93), which is what spots do.
As a control, the phases of all 24 sectors line up at 2P (resultant length 0.48,
Rayleigh p = 0.003) and do not line up at periods 1 % shorter or longer (0.27
and 0.17).

The package had called this "probably the host's rotation near a 2:1 spin–orbit
commensurability", from three sectors. With all of them it is not near. The
star's rotation has matched 2P to about one part in ten thousand for close to
three years. That is what tidal locking in a 3.874 d binary produces, and it is
not what a planet on a 1.937 d orbit would be expected to produce. This is the
vetter's second reason, measured here.

**2. The two dips at 2P are not the same depth.** Folded on one ephemeris, the
lab's doubled-period fold puts the same eclipse slot deeper in 21 of 24 sectors,
by 0.36 percentage points on average, on dips about 8.8 % deep. The formal
significance is 11σ, but the sectors scatter about twice as much as the
white-noise bars allow (χ² 96.7 for 23), which brings it to about 5σ. A second
estimate that shares none of the gate's machinery (one dip at a time on raw
flux, errors from the scatter between events) gives 0.21 percentage points in
the same direction, in 18 of 24 sectors, at about 3σ. Sectors 36, 37 and 38 read
the other way in both. Because of them the lab's combiner (`combine_p2_folds`)
fails its sign test and returns no verdict, which is what it is built to do.
SPOC's own odd/even comparison over all 259 transits reads no difference
(statistic 1.288). The lab's estimators and SPOC's do not agree, and this note
does not resolve why.

**3. The lab's own gates fire in three sectors, softly.** Sector by sector on the
fixed ephemeris, `odd_even_fold` returns `eclipsing-binary-odd-even` in sectors
62, 65 and 66, and `p2_fold` returns `eclipsing-binary-p2-alias` in sector 65
(5.0σ to 7.9σ on white-noise bars). The event-level estimate puts those three
sectors at 1.4σ, 2.0σ and 1.2σ. The firings are recorded because the contract's
exit condition is "every gate silent, on every sector", and on sectors nobody
had graded the gates were not silent. They are not, on their own, the reason
for this ruling. The output is a script's JSON on a fixed ephemeris, not a hunt
receipt.

The first measurement is the strong one. The second is a difference of the size
the vetter described. Neither is a mass.

## Why every gate stayed silent

- **The star was graded on 3 of its 24 sectors** (24 as of the 2026-09-18
  download). The shelf-exit contract (§3) says every archival sector must be
  pulled before a lead is minted, and marks that mint-time pull as not built.
  The other twenty-one were on disk from 2026-09-18. The lab's fold gates were
  never run on them until this note.
- **The fold gate asks one question: are the two eclipses different?** For
  TIC 287328866 the answer was yes, by 0.44 percentage points. For a
  close-to-equal pair the answer is "barely", and the gate has no second
  question. It does not look at the light between the dips, which is where this
  star gives itself away.
- **Physical admissibility graded the wrong radius.** `lab.a05_physical` computes
  the companion radius from the box-fit depth: 1.67 to 1.69 R_Jup (1.81 to 1.93
  after the crowding correction), under the 2.5 R_Jup ceiling. On a grazing
  eclipse the depth understates the size. SPOC's central value sits at the
  lab's own ceiling and the refit's whole range is over it, and neither number
  can reach the gate, because the shelf reads receipts and those numbers live in
  the package.
- **The ceiling does not know the host.** 2.5 R_Jup is one number for every star.
  The vetter's point is about M dwarfs specifically.
- **The secondary argument assumed the period it was testing.** See the precedent
  above.
- **Nothing reads Gaia's radial-velocity error.**
