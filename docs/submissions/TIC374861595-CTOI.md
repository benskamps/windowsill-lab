## 2 · Why it is a candidate and not yet a planet

**For.**
- Three independent sectors, 141-day baseline, period agreement to **0.7 minutes** across 33 transits (residuals 0.4, -0.7, 0.1 min).
- Depth consistent sector to sector (8.39 / 8.35 / 8.51 % ± 0.07).
- **Odd/even eclipses equal**: per sector 0.26σ, 0.59σ, 1.66σ; stacked 2.4σ on 1,088 in-transit points. Not an EB at 2P.
- **No secondary eclipse**: on undetrended flux with a per-event local baseline, 34 events, mean **−159 ± 628 ppm** (−0.3σ); 3σ upper limit ≈ **1.9 × 10³ ppm**. An M-dwarf stellar companion of this radius (~0.19 R☉, Teff ~2800 K) would show a ~2–3 % secondary in the TESS band. **A stellar companion is disfavoured.** A brown dwarf or giant planet (Teff ≲ 1500 K) is consistent.
- **On-target**: centroid shift 0.002–0.003 px in every sector; TIC contamination 0.07; nearest Gaia neighbours at 7.3″ (G 20.75) and 9.8″ (G 20.30) are 5 mag fainter — neither could produce a 9 % dip even eclipsing totally. Gaia RUWE 1.05.
- No TOI, no CTOI, not in the confirmed-planet table.

**Against, or unresolved.**
- **Size.** ~1.8–1.9 R_Jup around a 0.6 M☉ M dwarf. Known giant planets around M dwarfs are ≲1.3 R_Jup; brown dwarfs and the smallest stars are 0.9–1.2 R_Jup. 1.85 R_Jup is oversized for all three. Either R★ is smaller than TIC's 0.616 (Gaia colour bp_rp 2.47 and M_G 8.5 suggest an M2–M3 nearer 0.5 R☉, which would give ~1.5 R_Jup), or the object is an inflated giant, or the depth is inflated by unmodelled limb darkening in a trapezoid fit.
- **Shape density.** The circular-orbit density from the transit shape is 6.2 g/cm³ against TIC's 2.57 — a factor 2.4. Same direction as the size tension: the light curve wants a smaller, denser host than the catalogue. A limb-darkened fit and a proper R★ (Gaia-based) are the fix; neither is done here.
- **Trapezoid, not a physical model.** Every number above comes from a trapezoid with numpy; the round-bottomed fold (figure) shows limb darkening the model does not include. Depths are therefore slightly under, durations slightly over. Adequate for a candidate; a paper wants `batman`/`juliet` with a Gaia-anchored R★.
- **Nothing rules out a brown dwarf.** Only radial velocities can (K ≈ 300 m/s for 1 M_Jup, ≈ 15 km/s for 50 M_Jup, at this period on this host). Tmag 14.1 — feasible on a 4-m-class spectrograph.

**Verdict for the package:** *planet candidate (PC)* — giant planet or brown dwarf, ~1.8 R_Jup, P = 1.937 d, around an M2–M3 dwarf at 222 pc. Stellar companion disfavoured by the secondary-eclipse limit. Mass unknown.

## 3 · What "submit" now requires, in order

1. **A short peer-reviewed paper.** The natural venue is a Research Note of the AAS (≤1,000 words, one figure — the fold below fits) *if* RNAAS counts as peer-reviewed under ExoFOP's rule — check with ExoFOP first; otherwise AJ/MNRAS Letters. Content: §1 table, §2 evidence, the figure, and the receipts as the data statement.
2. **ExoFOP account** — "REQUEST AN ACCOUNT" on exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess (its own registration, not the Exoplanet Archive login).
3. **Published Candidate Upload Request** (`pub_candidate_upload_request.php`) with the paper URL.
4. **Upload** via `add_new.php?param=bulkparams` with the line in §1 and the tag.

Steps 2–4 are Ben's. Step 1 is writable from this package; the two upgrades that would make it referee-proof are a limb-darkened fit and a Gaia-based R★ — a day, not a week.

### 5b · Corrections to §1–2 that this forces

- **Ephemeris: adopt SPOC's.** Ours (1.9369093 ± 0.0000025 d over 141 d) and SPOC's differ by 3.4 s per cycle; our three mid-times sit at O−C = +5.2, +3.2, +0.8 min against SPOC's ephemeris — a monotone drift over 73 cycles. Either our trapezoid mid-times carry a bias the bootstrap cannot see, or the transit times vary. SPOC's 259-transit ephemeris is the one to quote; the O−C trend is a paper item, not a package item.
- **Depth and duration were underestimated by the trapezoid.** SPOC's limb-darkened fit: 10.46 % and 2.15 h against our 8.41 % and 1.57 h. The direction was predicted in §2 and the size is now measured.
- **The "twin stars excluded at 18σ" test is withdrawn as stated.** It compared a sharp V with a flat-topped trapezoid; a limb-darkened grazing transit is curved and beats both, so the test only excludes a *sharp* V, not a grazing geometry. SPOC's proper fit prefers **b = 0.90**. The flat-bottom argument does not carry the weight §2 gave it.
- **The shape density (6.1 g/cm³) is withdrawn.** With b = 0.9 the geometry is degenerate and the trapezoid's b ≈ 0.3 was the wrong branch. SPOC's a/R★ = 7.7 is consistent with the catalogue density.

### 5c · What still stands, and it is the load-bearing thing

**No secondary eclipse.** Ours: −159 ± 628 ppm over 34 events on undetrended flux, per-event local baselines. SPOC: max weak-secondary MES 3.3, 602 ± 168 ppm, not at phase 0.5. Two routes, same null.

At SPOC's k = 0.43, a luminous companion would show (TESS band, surface-brightness ratio ~ (T₂/T★)^3.5): M5 star **~14 %**; M7 star ~9 %; L dwarf ~2.8 %; T dwarf / old brown dwarf ~0.46 %; a planet at T_eq 745 K ~0.09 %. The 3σ limit is ~0.19 %. **Every stellar and L-type companion is excluded by two orders of magnitude.** What survives is an object cooler than ~1,000 K: a giant planet or a T-type brown dwarf. Since no such object is 2.6 R_Jup, SPOC's k is almost certainly the high end of its grazing degeneracy (±0.076 is 1σ; k ≈ 0.30 sits at 1.7σ and gives 1.8 R_Jup). **A grazing giant planet or cool brown dwarf, ~1.8–2.3 R_Jup, on a 1.937-day orbit around an M2–M3 dwarf.** Mass unknown; RV decides.

### 5d · Independent checks that came back clean

- PDM on raw PDCSAP (no BLS, no lab detrend): P = 1.93695 d. The two eclipses at 2P: depths 9.06 ± 0.07 vs 9.14 ± 0.07 % (0.8σ), mid-times +0.4 min apart, durations identical — the period is P, not 2P.
- Out-of-transit flux shows a coherent modulation at **3.80–3.87 d ≈ 2 P_orb**, semi-amplitude 0.6–1.6 % in all three sectors (this is why PDM's raw θ prefers 2P; the eclipse test above is what settles the period). Probably the host's rotation near a 2:1 spin–orbit commensurability; a paper item.
- Catalogues: SIMBAD (bare Gaia entry, no type), Gaia DR3 vari_summary / vari_eclipsing_binary / classifier / NSS all empty, RUWE 1.05, Prša+2022 TESS-EB catalogue absent, ExoFOP target page empty in every section, Exoplanet Archive TOI and PS tables empty, literature and web: nothing. **AAVSO VSX could not be queried programmatically (Cloudflare) — one browser check remains.**
- Host radius: Mann+2015 from M_K = 4.98 gives **0.616 R☉** (this is what TIC uses); Gaia GSP-Phot says 0.80 R☉ (model-based, unreliable for M dwarfs). Mann+2019 mass 0.72 M☉ → ρ 4.4 g/cm³. Use 0.62 R☉ and say why.

### 5e · What the package now says, honestly

Same disposition — **planet candidate** — with a sharper story than before: a strong, on-target, grazing transit that SPOC has seen 259 times and that was never promoted, whose *only* discriminating measurement is the missing secondary eclipse, which rules out a stellar companion and leaves a giant planet or cool brown dwarf. The paper writes itself around that one measurement plus an RV request. The upload line in §1 should carry SPOC's period, epoch, depth and duration, not ours.
