I ran the 2D q-state Potts model on an L=64 square lattice for q = 3, 4, 5, 6, each over a window straddling its exact critical temperature T_c(q) = 1/ln(1+√q). The Potts spin carries one of q flavours and a bond costs energy only when its two sites agree; the order parameter m = (q·ρ_max − 1)/(q − 1) melts from 1 (ordered) to 0 (disordered) as T rises, and its susceptibility peaks at T_c. The point of M07 is the *kind* of transition: continuous for q ≤ 4, first-order for q ≥ 5. Wall time on the GPU: 545s.
✓ Every q's susceptibility peak lands on its exact Potts T_c (±0.1 for the continuous q≤4, ±0.15 for the first-order q≥5, whose stronger finite-size effects shift the pseudo-critical peak further).
