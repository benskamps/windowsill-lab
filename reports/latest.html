2026-08-24 · M12 — 3D Edwards–Anderson spin glass
I ran the 3D Edwards–Anderson spin glass — Ising spins with quenched random ±J bonds on a simple-cubic lattice, E = −Σ J_ij s_i s_j — across lattice sizes L = [6, 8, 10, 12] on one shared temperature ladder [0.80, 1.60] straddling T_SG ≈ 0.95, averaged over 800 disorder realizations with two replicas each for the overlap q = (1/N) Σ s_i^α s_i^β. Unlike the 2D glass (M11, which orders only at T = 0), the 3D glass has a genuine finite-temperature transition, and its fingerprint is the disorder-averaged Binder cumulant crossing across sizes. Parallel tempering (mean swap acceptance 0.65) equilibrates the cold rungs. Wall time: 15858s.
✓ The Binder cumulant curves g_L(T) cross at T_SG = 1.124 — inside the 1.10 ± 0.10 benchmark band for the 3D ±J Edwards–Anderson transition — and the overlap stays symmetric (max|⟨q⟩| = 0.001 ≈ 0). Pairwise crossings [6/8 → 1.184, 6/10 → 1.070, 6/12 → 1.082, 8/10 → 0.973, 8/12 → 1.011, 10/12 → 1.124] (mean 1.074). This is the genuine finite-temperature spin-glass transition — the famous hard case that, unlike 2D (T_c = 0, M11), orders at a real temperature. The scale-invariant crossing (not a single-L peak) is the calibrated result.
Honesty on equilibration: 3D spin glasses are hard to equilibrate near T_SG (rugged landscape, long autocorrelation times). This engine uses checkerboard Metropolis with parallel tempering (replica exchange across the temperature ladder — the tool M11 lacked), because single-spin dynamics alone produces a smeared, crossing-free g_L(T) that only looks finished. The mean swap acceptance 0.65 and the symmetry diagnostic max|⟨q⟩| = 0.001 (≈ 0 when equilibrated) are reported so a mistune is visible, not hidden.
