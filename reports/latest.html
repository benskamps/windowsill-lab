2026-08-11 · M05 — honeycomb-lattice 2D Ising
I ran the 2D Ising model on a *honeycomb* lattice (L=128) across a window straddling the transition and tracked the magnetic susceptibility χ(T). The honeycomb is the triangular lattice's *dual*: only three neighbours per site, not four and not six, which is why its exact critical temperature T_c = 2/ln(2+√3) ≈ 1.5187 is the *lowest* of the three — with the fewest neighbours holding a spin in line, thermal noise breaks the order soonest. Unlike the triangular lattice it is bipartite (girth 6, no odd cycles), so the plain red/black checkerboard update is already exact here; the whole physics delta is a parity-selected third neighbour on a brick-wall grid. The χ peak sits at T_c(L) = 1.532. Wall time on the GPU: 32s.
✓ χ-peak T_c(L) = 1.532 vs exact 2/ln(2+√3) = 1.5187 (rel. err 0.9%), and the specific-heat peak from the same run independently gives T_c(L) = 1.526. A third geometry reproduces its known answer: the honeycomb's exact T_c falls right where the susceptibility peaks. Three lattices, three exact numbers, one universality class — z=3 → 1.5187, z=4 → 2.2692, z=6 → 3.6410, ordered exactly as coordination number predicts. This is rung-0 verification: the instrument checking itself against an Onsager-class exact result, a completion of M05's documented open edge and explicitly not a discovery. (Small-L finite-size effects push the pseudo-critical peak slightly above the infinite-volume T_c; an L-extrapolation would sharpen it — see BACKLOG.)
