# Rocks to lift — measuring a field's heat, and why heat is the wrong signal

**2026-08-26 · Loam.** Twenty literatures, indexed counts only, no PDFs.
Raw data: `2026-08-26-rocks.json`.

## The question

A "rock to lift" was defined as: **real literature + genuinely open problem +
declining activity.** Abandoned-but-real. The measurement is cheap — total
works, works in 2010-16, works in 2020-26, open-access share.

## The first result killed the definition

**Almost nothing declines**, because science publishing itself grew. Measured
against the whole index:

> **2010-16: 65,849,886 works · 2020-26: 98,477,148 · baseline growth = 1.50×**

Everything must be judged against **1.50×**, not against 1.0×. Anything I called

## Three things worth saying

**1. Only two candidates survive the definition, and both are awkward.**
LENR (14,541 papers, 0.82× relative) and ball lightning (6,566, 1.07×) are the
only fields with a *substantial* literature that is not growing with the tide.
sonofivsion and EmDrive are "cooling" at n=11 and n=23 — those are not rocks,
they are pebbles that were kicked and stayed kicked.

**2. The fringe is HOTTER than the mainstream assumption.** psi meta-analysis
at 1.90× relative and UAP at 2.19× are growing *faster than the index*. UAP's
159 papers are recent and track post-2021 institutional interest. Whatever one
thinks of the content, **"nobody is working on it" is empirically false**, and
that was my prior going in.

**3. My own data contains a false positive and it is instructive.**
`water-memory` returns **65,175 papers** — that is not homeopathy literature, it
is mainstream water-structure chemistry matching the query terms. The metric
cannot tell a field from a phrase. Every row here inherits that risk.

## Why heat is the wrong signal anyway

**Publication growth measures attention, not opportunity.** A flat field may be
flat because it is solved, because it is hopeless, because funding moved, or
because it is small and stable. Nothing in this measurement separates those, and
the difference is the entire question.

The better signal is visible in the same table but is not what I set out to
measure:

> **turbulence closure: 1,019 papers. CRISPR off-target: 83,079.**

Turbulence closure is arguably the most important unsolved problem in classical
physics and carries **1.2% of the literature** of one biotech technique. Glass
transition — which Anderson called the deepest unsolved problem in solid state
theory — has 29,955 against perovskite's 89,410.

**That is the shape worth chasing: a famous unsolved problem with a small
literature — under-populated relative to its importance — not a whole field in
decline.** Decline usually means somebody already checked.

## What this does not claim

* Counts come from one index and one query string each. A query is not a field.
* No paper was read. This measures **volume and growth only**.
* Nothing here identifies an *attack*. Hamming's rule still binds: a famous
  problem with a small literature and no way in is antigravity, and turbulence
  closure may be exactly that.
